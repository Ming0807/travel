# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Tourism staff, content administrators, provincial tourism planners, researchers, and system administrators use the platform to manage tourism content, QR check-in points, media, reports, and dashboard evidence for Yala, Pattani, and Narathiwat.

Tourists use the public PWA on mobile devices, often at an attraction with limited time and possibly weak internet. They need to understand the benefit quickly, create a certificate or travel memory with minimal data, and optionally share deeper travel behavior, expense, and satisfaction data after receiving value.

## Product Purpose

The platform exists to build a high-quality southern border tourism database for sustainable tourism planning. Public pages, QR flows, certificates, stamps, CMS tools, dashboards, and exports are supporting mechanisms for structured tourist data collection and decision-ready analysis.

Success means administrators can update public content without developer help, tourists can complete the QR-to-certificate flow with low friction, and planners can trust the resulting data for attraction, province, expense, satisfaction, and sustainability decisions.

## Brand Personality

Practical, trustworthy, place-aware.

The interface should feel like a serious civic tourism product: calm enough for repeated admin work, warm enough for destination storytelling, and precise enough for research-grade data management.

## Anti-references

Do not make the admin CMS feel like a generic CRUD scaffold, a decorative travel blog template, or a marketing landing page. Avoid sample content that looks real, oversized hero treatments inside tools, repeated card grids without task value, vague labels, hidden media ownership rules, and flows that force admins to remember where public-page content appears.

Do not make tourist-facing flows feel like a government data-collection form before the visitor receives value. Avoid forcing LINE, email, phone, national ID, full address, or long survey questions before certificate creation.

## Design Principles

1. Edit what visitors see: CMS editing should map to the public page sections and make the edited surface obvious.
2. Reward first, ask respectfully: tourist flows should collect minimal data before certificate or stamp value, then ask optional follow-up questions.
3. Govern media like public records: images need alt text, captions, credit, source, usage notes, lifecycle status, and archive behavior.
4. Prefer real connected data: public previews and homepage sections should use saved content relationships, not mock cards or confusing placeholders.
5. Show planning value: dashboards, lists, and exports should explain what each metric or record supports in tourism planning.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for contrast, focus visibility, keyboard access, readable Thai and English text, clear error recovery, and responsive layouts. Support reduced motion, avoid color-only status communication, and keep mobile tourist flows short enough for low-attention, low-bandwidth contexts.
